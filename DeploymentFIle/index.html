Why I built this

A friend's pharmacy was run on memory and paper.

A friend of mine runs a small neighborhood pharmacy. Like a lot of small shops, everything lived in his head and a stack of notebooks — what was in stock, what was running low, which strips were about to expire, and how much the shop had actually earned that day.

When he wanted to get organized, the software he found was the wrong fit: monthly subscriptions he didn't want to pay, cloud accounts that needed a steady internet connection he didn't always have, or heavy programs that crawled on the modest computer already sitting on his counter.

He didn't need any of that. He needed something free, something that runs on the low-power PC he already owns, and — most of all — a clear answer to one question: “how much did my shop make?” So I built him a desktop app that does exactly that.

What he actually needed

  • ✓Free of cost — no licence, no subscription.
  • ✓Runs on a slow, everyday computer.
  • ✓Works with no internet — data stays on the PC.
  • ✓One place to organize every product.
  • ✓Daily & monthly revenue at a glance.
The story

From a stock list to a real point-of-sale.

It started as a simple list of medicines. But a pharmacy isn't just a list — a sale has to find the right strip fast, take the money, and quietly do the right thing in the background. So it grew.

The first big decision was to make it local-first: the whole database is a single file on the shop's own hard drive (SQLite). That one choice solved three problems at once — it's free to run, it's fast on weak hardware, and it keeps working when the internet doesn't. The shop's data never has to leave the counter.

From there came the parts a real pharmacy lives on: first-expiry-first-out selling so the oldest stock goes first and nothing quietly expires on the shelf; a prescription gate so Rx-only medicines can't be sold without a verified prescription; and batch & expiry tracking with low-stock warnings. To save hours of typing, I bundled the entire ~21,000-medicine national catalogue offline, searchable instantly — the owner just picks what he stocks.

And running through all of it: a revenue dashboard. Today's takings, the month so far, best-sellers, payment mix — the answer to his original question, on the screen the moment he opens the app.

Purpose · who it's for

Made for the small, independent pharmacy.

The kind of shop run by one owner and a counter assistant or two — that needs to be organized and profitable without paying for enterprise software or fighting a slow internet line.

The shop owner

Sees revenue, stock value, what's selling and what's expiring — and decides what to reorder. Admin role unlocks settings, staff and reports.

The counter staff

A fast, locked-down sell screen: search, add to cart, take cash/bKash/Nagad/card, print the receipt. They see only what they need.

The patients

Prescription-only medicines are gated behind a verified prescription, expired stock is never sold, and VIP customers get refill reminders.

Tech stack

Chosen to be free, fast and offline.

⚡Electron

Packages the app as a real Windows program that bundles its own runtime — the shop installs one .exe, nothing else.

⚛️React + Vite + TypeScript

The interface. TypeScript keeps a money-handling app honest; Vite keeps the UI snappy even on weak hardware.

🗄️SQLite (better-sqlite3)

The entire database is one local file — zero setup, tiny footprint, and instant reads on a low-power PC. This is what makes it free & offline.

🔎SQLite FTS5

Full-text search powers instant lookups across the ~21,000-medicine catalogue without a server.

🛡️Secure IPC bridge

The UI never touches the database directly — every request crosses a guarded contextBridge layer where permissions are enforced.

📦electron-builder

Produces the signed-installer-ready Windows .exe automatically via GitHub Actions — no Windows machine needed to ship.

How it's built

A clean, local-first architecture.

Four honest layers. The database is owned by the main process and only ever reached through a typed, permission-checked bridge.

🖥️ Main

src/main

Electron + SQLite. Owns the data, business rules (FEFO, Rx gate), and the offline catalogue.

🔌 Preload

src/preload

A secure contextBridge — the only door between the UI and the database.

🎨 Renderer

src/renderer

React screens: POS, Dashboard, Inventory, Prescriptions, People, Reports, Settings.

🤝 Shared

src/shared

One source of truth for domain types, the IPC contract and the roles/permissions matrix.
